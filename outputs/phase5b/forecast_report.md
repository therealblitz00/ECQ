# Phase 5b: explainable sales and price estimates

Generated by `src/phase5b_forecast.py run` (2026-10-28). Products are colourways; tests use only models never sold in the training seasons (new products); intervals are 95% bootstrap over models.

## Protocol

- **Sales = whole-life units.** Sales are accumulated until the extraction (about January 2025): seasons 232 and 241 had finished selling, 242 had not (table below). So the units are learned and scored only on whole lives: **train 232 → the new models of 241**, split at random by model into two halves: one for the choices (validation), one reported once (test).
- **242 for sales: ranking only.** Its sales are cut by the extraction, so they never score the units; they only check the ranking among products of a category launched in the same month (equally cut), with a model trained on 232 + 241.
- **No time on sale.** The days to the extraction describe the extraction, not the product (and fall outside the training range for a new season). The planned launch month (known when ordering) stays.
- **Price = landed cost × markup.** The price model estimates the markup over the landed cost (`PRICE_LCP`, known when the price is set); the cost carries the rise of prices between seasons, which trees cannot extrapolate. Products without a cost use the model without it. **Validation 232 → 241, test 232 + 241 → 242** (list prices are not cut by the extraction).
- **Left out:** buyer decisions (`PUR_BET`, `TYPOLOGY`; see `buyer_report.md`); sales are never a feature; the price model never sees the price or the numeric similarity block.
- **Features**, by a rule declared before the choice: a block leaves only if it helps in neither check (whole lives 232 → 241, and the same-month ranking on 242). Description components, materials and launch month help in neither and leave both estimates. The photo components stay for **sales**: neutral on 241 (−0.04 to +0.02 same-month ranking) but +0.005 to +0.052 on 242, where the model trains on twice the products; this uses the 242 check, so it is stated. **Price**: attributes only (the photo helps in neither; `feature_selection.md`).
- **A choice** (the similar products as a feature) is kept only if the 95% interval of its validation gain excludes 0.

## The delivered estimates (`data/processed/forecast.parquet`)

Never in-sample: products with a known whole-life sales or price get a **cross-validated** estimate (5 folds grouped by model, so neither the product nor its other colours were seen); the others (new products, 242 products whose sales were cut, products never sold) a forecast from all usable products. Sales are **whole-life units** (median-type estimate: exp of the mean log); price is landed cost × markup, also snapped to the nearest list price (`price_point_estimate`). **Typical error measured on the test split:** sales × 1.69 either way (exp of the mean absolute log error; 76% of the products within a factor 2), price ± 2.67 €. Units per product only: the data has no store or week breakdown.

**80% range** per product (`sales_low`/`sales_high`, `price_low`/`price_high`): the estimate times the 10th and 90th percentiles of real / estimate on the test split (sales ×0.50 to ×2.33, price ×0.89 to ×1.23). A readable copy for every product, with the description, is `outputs/phase5b/estimates.csv`.

## How the explanations are computed

- **Gradient boosting** (LightGBM): exact TreeSHAP values (`pred_contrib`), adding up to the estimate, milliseconds per product; grouped into business families because single principal components mean nothing.
- **Similar past products**: their similarity-weighted mean, so the products themselves are the explanation. Caveat: the engine's weights were tuned in Phase 4 with an objective that includes the sales correlation, so this reference is slightly optimistic. The delivered sales reference averages whole-life products only.
